Common Analytical Framework

Across technical and non-technical work, I apply the same underlying analytical process.

01

Observe

Begin with the system, event or problem as it exists rather than relying on initial assumptions.

02

Define the Question

Clarify the issue being investigated, including its scope, context, constraints and uncertainty.

03

Collect Evidence

Review theory, case studies, technical data, historical context and relevant supporting research.

04

Challenge Assumptions

Examine whether an initial explanation remains valid when compared against alternative interpretations.

05

Recognise Patterns

Identify relationships between technology, behaviour, institutions, incentives, history and system outcomes.

06

Draw a Reasoned Conclusion

Form a supported conclusion while acknowledging limitations, uncertainty and the boundaries of the available evidence.

Why Governance, Behaviour and Society Matter to an Engineer

Constitution and Institutions

Engineering programmes are influenced by institutions that establish priorities, allocate resources, regulate safety and remain accountable to the public. My broader interest in constitutional studies and governance helps me examine how institutions shape technical development and public outcomes.

Human Behaviour and Security

Technology is designed, operated and sometimes misused by people. Behaviour, group influence, incentives and social conditions can therefore affect the performance and consequences of technical systems. My academic work in criminal psychology provided a framework for examining these human factors in relation to radicalisation and organised violence.

Human Rights and Accountability

Engineering in defence, autonomy, surveillance and public infrastructure requires more than technical performance. It also requires legal boundaries, institutional accountability and awareness of how decisions affect individuals and communities. My human-rights research strengthened my ability to evaluate competing claims, institutional responsibility and the difference between principles and their practical enforcement.

Community Building and National Development

Technology contributes to nation-building when it improves capability, resilience, access, security and opportunity. I am interested in how engineering can support this process while remaining connected to responsible governance, social cohesion and long-term public value.

Case File 001

Human Rights, Universality and Political Selectivity

Completed
Central Question: Are human rights genuinely universal, or are modern human-rights systems shaped by Western political history and selective institutional enforcement?

This research examined the distinction between the universal moral aspiration of human rights and the political reality of how rights are interpreted, prioritised and enforced. The paper considered the historical development of modern human-rights frameworks while assessing whether institutions apply those principles consistently across different communities and political contexts.

The analysis drew upon historical and contemporary case studies, including Noakhali, the exodus of Kashmiri Hindus and the 26/11 Mumbai attacks, to examine institutional accountability, political attention and the limits of universal enforcement.

Case File 002

Criminal Psychology and Radicalisation

Completed
Central Question: How can behavioural and criminological theory help explain the process through which an individual becomes involved in organised terrorism?

This research applied criminal-psychology and criminological theories to the case of Ajmal Kasab and the 26/11 Mumbai attacks. The objective was not simply to describe the event, but to investigate the behavioural mechanisms that may contribute to recruitment, indoctrination, radicalisation and participation in organised violence.

The study applied Social Learning Theory, Strain Theory and Social Control Theory to examine how environment, grievance, group influence, weakened social bonds and exposure to violent norms may interact within the radicalisation process.
