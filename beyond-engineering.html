Systems Thinking Beyond Aerospace Engineering

Interdisciplinary Analysis, Research and Pattern Recognition

While aerospace engineering remains the foundation of my academic and professional direction, I have also been interested in understanding complex systems beyond technical design. Through academic work in criminal psychology and international human rights, I explored how structured analysis can be applied to human behaviour, institutions, law, history and public policy. Although these disciplines differ from engineering, the analytical process remains remarkably similar: observe carefully, define the problem, examine evidence, compare explanations and understand how multiple variables interact before reaching a conclusion.

Common Analytical Framework

Across both technical and non-technical work, I tend to follow the same underlying process.

01

Observe

Begin with the system, event or problem as it exists rather than relying on initial assumptions.

02

Define the Question

Clarify the exact issue being investigated, including scope, context, constraints and uncertainty.

03

Collect Evidence

Review theory, case studies, primary material, historical context and relevant supporting research.

04

Challenge Assumptions

Examine whether the initial explanation remains valid when compared against alternative interpretations.

05

Recognise Patterns

Identify recurring relationships between behaviour, institutions, incentives, history and system outcomes.

06

Draw a Reasoned Conclusion

Form a supported position while acknowledging limitations, uncertainty and areas where further evidence may change the interpretation.

Case File 001

Human Rights, Universality and Political Selectivity

Completed
Central Question: Are human rights genuinely universal, or are modern human-rights systems shaped by Western political history and selective institutional enforcement?

This research examined the difference between the universal moral aspiration of human rights and the political reality of how those rights are interpreted, prioritised and enforced. The study considered the historical development of modern human-rights frameworks while evaluating whether institutions apply those principles consistently across different communities, states and political contexts.

The analysis used historical and contemporary examples including Noakhali, the exodus of Kashmiri Hindus and the 26/11 Mumbai attacks to examine institutional accountability, political attention and the practical limits of universal enforcement.

Domain International Human Rights
Analytical Focus Universality and Institutional Accountability
Method Comparative Historical and Legal Analysis
Case Studies Noakhali, Kashmiri Hindu Exodus and 26/11

Case File 002

Criminal Psychology and Radicalisation

Completed
Central Question: How can behavioural and criminological theory help explain the process through which an individual becomes involved in organised terrorism?

This research applied criminal-psychology and criminological theories to the case of Ajmal Kasab and the 26/11 Mumbai attacks. The objective was not simply to describe the attack, but to investigate the behavioural mechanisms that may contribute to recruitment, indoctrination, radicalisation and participation in organised violence.

The study applied Social Learning Theory, Strain Theory and Social Control Theory to examine how environment, grievance, group influence, weakened social bonds and exposure to violent norms may interact within the radicalisation process.

Domain Criminal Psychology
Analytical Focus Radicalisation and Behavioural Influence
Method Theory-Based Case Analysis
Theoretical Framework Social Learning, Strain and Social Control Theory

One Event, Two Analytical Perspectives

The 26/11 Mumbai attacks appeared in both studies, but each investigation asked a fundamentally different question. This comparison demonstrates how one event can be examined at individual, institutional and societal levels without reducing it to a single explanation.

Criminal Psychology Perspective

  • How does individual radicalisation develop?
  • What role do recruitment, indoctrination and group influence play?
  • How do grievance, environment and weakened social bonds affect behaviour?
  • How can behavioural theory explain participation in organised violence?

Human Rights and Institutional Perspective

  • How should institutions balance justice, security and rights?
  • How is accountability pursued across legal and political boundaries?
  • Why do some victims or events receive greater institutional attention?
  • How can communities be protected without encouraging collective blame?

Pattern Recognition

Across both studies, I found myself less interested in treating events as isolated incidents and more interested in identifying the relationships that shape them.

Interacting Variables

Complex outcomes rarely emerge from one cause. They develop through interaction between individual choices, social environments, institutions, incentives and historical conditions.

Competing Explanations

A useful analysis should test more than one interpretation rather than selecting the explanation that most closely matches an initial belief.

Institutions and Feedback

Institutional responses can reduce a problem, reinforce it or create unintended effects depending on how authority, incentives and accountability are structured.

Limits of Simplification

Technical and social systems both become misleading when reduced to a single variable without considering context, interaction and uncertainty.

Connecting Engineering and Human Systems

The subjects differ, but both require attention to inputs, interactions, failure mechanisms and validation.

Engineering Systems

Inputs Loads, commands and disturbances
Interactions Structures, controls and autonomy
Failure Modes Instability, fatigue and poor integration

Human and Institutional Systems

Inputs History, incentives and environment
Interactions People, institutions and narratives
Failure Modes Radicalisation and weak accountability
Validation Evidence, theory and case comparison
